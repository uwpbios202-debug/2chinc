The problem

Who does what: you before the call, counsel after.

You can do, with our guidance

  • Order certified death certificates
  • List every heir and their mailing address
  • Search recorded documents for each parcel
  • Gather assets, debts and creditors
  • Send notices by certified mail and keep the receipts

Only counsel can do

  • Judge which route fits
  • Certify and file the petition
  • Advise on risk and appear in court
  • Approve the final deed
Honest limit: preparation shortens the first list. It never replaces the second.

Stage two

Probate is one route, not the assumption.

RouteWhen it may fit
Independent administrationSomeone needs authority to act for the estate.
Supervised administrationA dispute or protected interest calls for court oversight.
Heirship order onlyThe missing piece is a court finding of who the heirs are.
Summary administrationGross estate of $100,000 or less, every heir consents, other conditions met.
Title-company routeAn insurer accepts the heirship evidence in writing.
Title or ownership disputeCompeting deeds or co-owner disagreements need counsel.

We help you ask which route fits. Your lawyer decides.

Stage four

Six questions to ask before the clock starts.

  1. Which route do you recommend, and why not the others?
  2. Will you file what we prepared, and what must you change?
  3. What is covered, at what price for each part, in writing?
  4. Can any fees come from the estate once we have access?
  5. Will the title company insure without letters, and on what terms?
  6. What can we keep doing ourselves to cut your hours?

Question 3 should cover review, filing, bond, first hearing, letters, creditor notices and later work.
