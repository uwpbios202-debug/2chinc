Living options

Ways to live, and the help that can come with them

There are many ways to live, with more or less help. This page explains the main ones so you can explore them. You choose.

This is not a diagnosis, and nothing here is a recommendation. The Mirror never picks a place for you, and it never sends anyone to assisted living or a nursing home. What you can do, what you would like, and where you want to live are yours to say.

How to use this page

  • Read about any option. Most people mix and match: their own place, plus some help that comes to them.
  • In your own Mirror, under Where I live, you can tick the options you would like to learn more about.
  • Sometimes someone helping you marks "professional review needed". That means a trained professional, such as a nurse, a social worker or an options counselor, could help you look at your choices. It is not a decision, it does not move you anywhere, and you still choose.

Where to ask, at no cost

  • 2-1-1 (call or text 211): help finding housing and support programs.
  • Aging and Disability Resource Center (ADRC) of Milwaukee County: for older adults and adults with disabilities, free advice about living options, help at home, and programs such as Family Care and IRIS.
  • Your coordinator at the organization you work with.

The options

  • Living on my own. Your own apartment or house, rented or owned, run your way.
  • My own place, with support that comes to me. Your own home, with help that visits you there.
  • Assisted living. A home with your own room or apartment, where help is there every day.
  • Nursing home (skilled nursing care). Nursing care around the clock, for a short stay or longer.
  • Accessible housing. A home built or changed to fit your body and your needs.
  • Permanent supportive housing. Your own lease, with rent help and support services, and no time limit.
  • Other specialized housing. Homes for a particular group or purpose.

For what each option is, what help comes with it, how it is paid for and questions to ask, turn on JavaScript, or ask 2-1-1 or your coordinator.

The words "who it may suit" describe who often chooses an option. They are not rules, and only a program itself can say who qualifies.
