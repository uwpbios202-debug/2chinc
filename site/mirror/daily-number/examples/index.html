What a $0 Day Can Look Like in Milwaukee’s Woods

Some people living in wooded areas of Milwaukee get through a day without spending cash. This is an illustrative day. It shows what someone may have to build, find, carry, or go without. The $46.98 above belongs to the supported budget. The outdoor column describes how needs are met or go unmet, with no dollar estimate attached.

Two ways a day can show $0 out of pocket
Daily needOthers cover the bills (monthly example)A $0 day in Milwaukee’s woods
Shelter$650.00 · A host provides a place to stay.Someone builds a makeshift shelter from discarded tarps, pallets, or other salvaged materials. Keeping it dry, warm, and intact takes ongoing work.
Utilities and water$75.00 · A host covers utilities.There may be no electricity, plumbing, or indoor heat. Daylight shapes the day. Water has to be found and carried.
Food$320.00 · Community support covers meals and groceries.Food might come from what can be gathered from the land or from what restaurants discard. What is available, safe to eat, and enough for the day can all be uncertain.
Getting around$99.00 · Help covers bus fare.Walking may be the only option. Some people have a bicycle. Distance, weather, and what someone can carry shape which places they can reach.
Health and medicine$75.00 · Help covers health costs.Getting to care, keeping medication on hand, and finding a place to recover can be difficult. Care may be delayed or go unmet.
Phone and internet$25.00 · Help covers phone service.A phone, if someone has one, still needs charging and a connection. Missing a call can mean missing an appointment or a housing referral.
Clothing and personal care$100.00 · Supplies or gifts cover these costs.Salvaged or donated clothes may need drying, repair, or replacement. Finding somewhere to wash and use a restroom takes time.
Household and laundry$35.00 · Help covers supplies and laundry.Keeping bedding clean and dry with no reliable laundry access can take repeated trips and labor.
Emergency reserve$50.00 · A gift funds the reserve.There may be no cash cushion when a bicycle breaks, bedding gets soaked, or shelter materials need replacing.

What support would help someone build the life they choose? At 2nd Chance Housing, we understand how much skill it takes to get through a day this way. We’re still learning how to connect people with the support they want and can actually use. That starts with asking what would help them turn the resourcefulness they already have into lasting stability and more control over their lives in Milwaukee.

Where the numbers come from

Transit. The $99 monthly budget uses the adult WisGo fare cap. Check the current fare and how to qualify for fare capping. Milwaukee County Transit System

Housing, groceries, health costs and other amounts. These are planning assumptions, primarily drawn from the project’s working drafts. The $1,200 one-bedroom amount is an illustrative rent-and-utilities budget. Actual rents depend on the unit and location. HUD Fair Market Rent data · USDA monthly food cost reports

Monthly amounts are annualized before rounding the daily figures. The examples work on this device and goal edits reset when you reload.
