The Daily Number

Know your cost. Understand your resources. Build your future.

365 days. One life. A plan for every day. The Daily Number is what one day of your life costs, on average.

Every person has a daily cost of living, even with no income, no savings and no home: the cost of living in a city does not become zero because income is zero. Thinking one day at a time makes that cost easier to understand, because a day is how we live.

Daily Number = what your life costs in a year ÷ 365. A $600 yearly insurance bill is not $600 a month; it adds about $1.64 to each day.

Three numbers, not one

  • Survival: what it costs to stay safe, fed, clean, connected and alive for one day. The immediate minimum.
  • Stability: what it costs to keep a secure, independent life: rent, utilities, food, getting around, phone, health care, savings.
  • The life you want: stability plus your own goals: learning, family, interests, savings, a business, giving back.

These are not standards of human worth. They are different money situations, and the possibilities in each.

Cost is not the same as income

Benefits, housing help, SNAP and donated things lower what you pay, but they are not cash income, and the Mirror keeps them apart. If life costs $75 a day after taxes, you may need to earn more than $75 a day before taxes. You don't have to earn every dollar: affordable housing, health coverage, benefits and community help all lower the cash you need.
